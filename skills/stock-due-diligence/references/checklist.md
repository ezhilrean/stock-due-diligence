# Stock Due-Diligence Checklist

Use this as an investigation checklist, not as a claim that every item has been conclusively cleared. Mark each item `Reviewed`, `Flag`, `Not found in sources reviewed`, `Not applicable`, or `Not verified`. A checked item means it was reviewed, not necessarily cleared.

## A. Identity and business
- [ ] Confirm legal entity, ticker, exchange, share class, and listing jurisdiction.
- [ ] Confirm reporting currency, fiscal year, and analysis date.
- [ ] Understand products, services, revenue model, customers, and markets.
- [ ] Identify major customers, suppliers, competitors, and dependencies.
- [ ] Review subsidiaries, joint ventures, and related-party entities.
- [ ] Assess competitive advantages and business sustainability.

## B. Financial health and accounting
- [ ] Revenue, profit, margins, and multi-year trends.
- [ ] Operating cash flow and free cash flow.
- [ ] Cash, debt, interest coverage, liquidity, and working capital.
- [ ] Accounts receivable relative to revenue.
- [ ] Inventory relative to sales.
- [ ] Auditor opinion, qualifications, and auditor changes.
- [ ] Restatements, policy changes, or unusual adjustments.
- [ ] Profit versus cash-flow consistency.
- [ ] Share-based compensation and dilution.
- [ ] Unusual expenses, transactions, guarantees, and liabilities.

## C. Legal and regulatory
- [ ] Civil litigation and shareholder actions.
- [ ] Criminal investigations, charges, or convictions.
- [ ] Securities, accounting, bribery, corruption, and insider-trading matters.
- [ ] Regulatory enforcement, fines, sanctions, and license issues.
- [ ] Tax disputes and liabilities.
- [ ] Labor, safety, consumer, environmental, competition, and antitrust matters.
- [ ] Intellectual-property disputes.
- [ ] Cases involving executives, directors, founders, and controlling shareholders.
- [ ] Record each matter's status, date, outcome, financial exposure, and company response.

## D. Fraud, transparency, and disclosure
- [ ] Verify material claims about contracts, customers, products, and growth.
- [ ] Check related-party transactions and conflicts of interest.
- [ ] Check for undisclosed liabilities, guarantees, and material risks.
- [ ] Compare filings with company announcements and independent sources.
- [ ] Review filing delays, disclosure gaps, and unexplained inconsistencies.
- [ ] Review insider sales in context; do not infer wrongdoing from sales alone.
- [ ] Assess credible whistleblower, investigative-journalism, and short-seller claims.
- [ ] Distinguish evidence from allegation and speculation.

## E. Cybersecurity and IT integrity
- [ ] Confirmed breaches, data leaks, ransomware, or other security incidents.
- [ ] Customer-account integrity, transaction accuracy, and record reliability.
- [ ] Regulatory findings and data-protection compliance.
- [ ] Material IT-control weaknesses and audit findings.
- [ ] Repeated outages and operational disruption.
- [ ] Customer impact, remediation, and independent assurance.
- [ ] Do not label an incident malware or deliberate manipulation without evidence.

## F. Management and governance
- [ ] Leadership history and substantiated misconduct.
- [ ] Board independence and effectiveness.
- [ ] Auditor independence.
- [ ] Executive compensation versus performance.
- [ ] Ownership, voting rights, and minority-shareholder protections.
- [ ] Insider holdings and transactions.
- [ ] Whistleblower systems and internal controls.
- [ ] Management guidance versus outcomes.

## G. Valuation and business outlook
- [ ] Use valuation metrics appropriate to the sector.
- [ ] Compare with company history and relevant peers.
- [ ] Review cash-flow assumptions and capital needs.
- [ ] Consider optimistic, base, and pessimistic scenarios.
- [ ] Estimate downside, upside, and margin of safety.
- [ ] Assess growth sustainability, capital allocation, and dilution.
- [ ] Consider competition, disruption, regulation, macroeconomics, and geopolitics.

## H. Trading and shareholder risks
- [ ] Liquidity and likely transaction impact.
- [ ] Volatility, trading halts, and exchange compliance.
- [ ] Unusual price or volume patterns.
- [ ] Short interest and allegations, with corroboration.
- [ ] Share issuance, buybacks, lock-up expirations, and insider sales.
- [ ] Delisting, restructuring, and bankruptcy risk.
- [ ] Dividend coverage and tax implications, where relevant.

## I. Evidence quality
- [ ] Primary sources reviewed first where available.
- [ ] Important claims have source links and dates.
- [ ] Legal and regulatory status is current as of the report date.
- [ ] Conflicting evidence is described.
- [ ] Missing information is explicitly disclosed.
- [ ] No unsupported claim that a company is “clean,” “fraudulent,” or “safe.”
